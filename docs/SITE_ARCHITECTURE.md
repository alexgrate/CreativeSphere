# THE CREATIVE SPHERE — SITE ARCHITECTURE

*Companion to CREATIVE_DIRECTION_V2.md. The creative direction defines the feeling; this document defines the structure that keeps the project from being a beautiful, empty tech demo.*

---

## The Core Decision: Trailer + Worlds

The website is **not a one-page site**. It is a cinematic home experience plus real, content-rich pages — all living in the same universe.

**The Journey** (`/`) is the trailer: the 8-scene scroll experience from the creative direction. Its job is emotional — make the visitor *feel* the agency's capability in 2–3 minutes and pull them deeper.

**The Worlds** are the substance: real routed pages with real content. Their job is commercial — convince, inform, rank on Google, and close.

This is the exact pattern of the sites we benchmark: Lusion's home is spectacle; their work pages are meaty. The spectacle earns attention; the content earns trust.

## One Universe, Two Modes

The pages must never feel like a different website bolted onto the journey. Rules:

1. **Same matter everywhere.** Every page keeps the darkness, a calm ambient particle field (lighter than the journey's), the same typography and light language. Pages are *locations* in the universe, not exits from it.
2. **The journey links outward at story beats.** Scene 4 (constellations) offers the path to `/services`. Scene 5 (galaxy) offers `/work`. Scene 8's CTA leads to `/contact`. The trailer feeds the worlds.
3. **Every deep page can send you back to the journey** — the star map is global.
4. **Editorial premium, not carnival.** On content pages, motion supports reading: reveal on scroll, parallax imagery, elegant type. No scroll hijacking on content pages. Content pages are where restraint proves the agency is a grown-up.

---

## Why This Structure (Anti-Flop Reasoning)

- **SEO reality.** A one-pager can rank for one thing. Service pages + case studies + about can rank for dozens of intents ("branding agency…", "web design for…", client names). This is where agency leads actually come from.
- **The send-back problem.** After a pitch, a prospect returns to *check something specific*. They need `/work/client` to link a colleague — not "scroll to 62% of the journey."
- **Depth signals.** Buyers judge agencies by case study depth (numbers, process, artifacts) and by the team page. A site with no faces and no numbers reads as a freelancer with good shaders.
- **Scope survival.** The journey alone is months of WebGL craft. Content pages are conventional builds — they can ship early and earn while the spectacle is perfected.

---

## Content Inventory (what the company must supply)

The build can fake none of this. Needed, in priority order:

1. **3–6 case studies**: client name & permission, the challenge, the approach, deliverables, visuals (images/video), and at least one real metric each (growth %, launch results, rankings).
2. **Six service definitions**: what's included, typical process, example deliverables, starting engagement shape.
3. **Company story**: founding, mission, the team (names, roles, photos).
4. **Proof**: testimonials, logos, stats for Scene 7's satellites (projects completed, industries served, campaigns launched, brands transformed) — real numbers only.
5. **Brand kit**: logo SVG, typeface, any existing brand rules.
6. **Voice**: who writes the copy? The journey's lines are load-bearing; placeholder copy cannot ship.

---

## Build Order (ship-early strategy)

1. **Phase A — The Journey skeleton** *(in progress)*: spine, chapters, Scene 1, particle engine.
2. **Phase B — The content core**: routing, page shells, one complete case study, services, about, contact. The site becomes *sendable* and indexable here, even while the journey is half-dressed.
3. **Phase C — Scene-by-scene dressing**: journey scenes to full beauty, linked to their pages.
4. **Phase D — The connective tissue**: star map, threshold, sound, second-visit behavior, polish.

A premium site that ships beats a legendary site that doesn't.
